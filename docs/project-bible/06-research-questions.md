# Research Questions

- **Status:** Draft v1.1 — Awaiting human review
- **Owner:** Research Lead
- **Version:** 1.1
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-19
- **Purpose:** Define the behavioral questions the public-research corpus must address.

## Primary question

What prevents or enables customers and quick-commerce shoppers to purchase from a lifetime-new or dormant category during a month?

**Monthly Active Customer is the business-metric population, not a public-research sampling criterion.** Public evidence cannot verify whether an author qualifies as a Monthly Active Customer.

Primary scope is category-level expansion. Product-level trial is considered only when it explains category entry, abandonment, purchase, purchase elsewhere, workaround, or repeat behavior. Flavors, brands, pack sizes, and variants within familiar categories are excluded unless directly relevant to a category-expansion mechanism.

## Shopping missions and habits

1. What shopping missions bring customers to quick-commerce services, and which missions leave room for category exploration?
2. When do customers follow a habitual list versus browse beyond an intended purchase?
3. How does urgency affect attention, comparison, and willingness to experiment?
4. What prior experiences reinforce staying within familiar categories?

## Category consideration

5. What causes an unfamiliar category to enter a customer's consideration set?
6. At what point is a category ignored, evaluated, postponed, or rejected?
7. Which category characteristics appear to change willingness to experiment?
8. How do lifetime-new and previously purchased but dormant categories differ behaviorally?

## Trust and perceived risk

9. What functional, financial, safety, quality, authenticity, or social risks do customers describe?
10. Which trust signals matter before purchasing in an unfamiliar category?
11. When is platform trust sufficient, and when is product, brand, seller, or community trust needed?
12. Which categories appear to carry consequences that make trial feel unacceptable?

## Information needs and decision criteria

13. What information do customers seek before trying a new category or product type?
14. Which criteria determine whether they proceed, compare elsewhere, or abandon?
15. Where do customers look when the current shopping surface does not answer their questions?
16. How does information need differ between routine, urgent, and high-consideration missions?

## Workarounds and alternatives

17. What do customers do when they do not purchase the category through quick commerce?
18. Which alternate channels, communities, people, or tools do they use, and why?
19. Do workarounds solve an information, trust, availability, price, or other gap?

## Triggers and experiment behavior

20. What situational, product, social, price, or lifecycle triggers lead to category experimentation?
21. What makes a first trial feel sufficiently low-risk?
22. What post-purchase outcomes encourage or discourage repeat category purchase?
23. When does an incentive create meaningful trial versus one-time discount seeking?

## Segments and mental models

24. Which observable contexts produce meaningfully different behavior without inferring unsupported demographics?
25. What mental models do customers express about what Zepto or quick commerce is appropriate for?
26. How do these mental models change after a successful or unsuccessful category purchase?

## Contradictory and disconfirming questions

27. Who purchases unfamiliar categories without hesitation, and what makes their context different?
28. Where is detailed information unnecessary or harmful to shopping speed?
29. Which high-perceived-risk categories still achieve experimentation, and why?
30. When do customers prefer quick commerce over specialist marketplaces for unfamiliar categories?
31. What evidence contradicts the idea that trust, information, price, or urgency is the main barrier?
32. Which apparent barriers are complaints without observable effect on purchase behavior?
33. Are there cases where discovery increases browsing but not purchase or repeat purchase?

## Interpretation rule

A research question is not evidence. Answers remain open until supported by linked, reviewed public evidence and bounded by corpus limitations.
