# Behavioral Codebook v1

- **Status:** Draft v1 — Awaiting human review
- **Owner:** Product Research Lead
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-19
- **Purpose:** Define consistent behavioral codes for turning public discussions into inspectable evidence units.

## Evidence warning

All examples below are constructed, illustrative examples. They are not user quotes, collected evidence, Zepto findings, or claims about actual customer behavior.

## Coding rules

- Code only what the source supports in context.
- Mark each coded value as **explicit** or **inferred**.
- Use `unknown` when context is insufficient.
- Do not infer demographics, motivation, causality, or outcomes from sentiment alone.
- One evidence item may receive several codes when each is independently supported.
- Preserve the neutral observation separately from the researcher's interpretation.

## Code definitions

### Barrier

- **Definition:** A condition that prevents, delays, or makes category consideration or purchase harder.
- **Include when:** The source connects the condition to avoidance, postponement, comparison, abandonment, or reduced confidence.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper leaves to compare baby-product specifications because the available details do not answer a safety question.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** A shopper says the app color is unattractive without connecting it to category behavior.
- **Do not assume:** Every complaint is a purchase barrier.

### Trigger

- **Definition:** An event, need, cue, or context that initiates category consideration or experimentation.
- **Include when:** A specific situation precedes and plausibly prompts consideration or purchase.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** Running out of pet food late at night prompts a first quick-commerce pet-care purchase.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** A product was visible, but the source does not say visibility affected consideration.
- **Do not assume:** Temporal sequence proves causality.

### Habit

- **Definition:** A repeated behavior or default response performed with limited reconsideration in a recurring context.
- **Include when:** Repetition, routine, default channel, or automatic list behavior is explicit or strongly contextualized.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper routinely reorders the same grocery list and does not browse categories.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** One repeat purchase with no evidence of a stable routine.
- **Do not assume:** Frequency alone establishes a habit loop.

### Workaround

- **Definition:** An alternative action used to complete the job when the current journey does not meet a need.
- **Include when:** The source describes another channel, person, tool, delay, or manual process used instead.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper checks a specialist marketplace for reviews before returning to complete the purchase.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** The shopper mentions using another marketplace but gives no connection to the current decision.
- **Do not assume:** Every competitor purchase is a workaround.

### Decision criterion

- **Definition:** An attribute or threshold used to choose, reject, or compare an option.
- **Include when:** The source identifies what mattered to the decision.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** The shopper chooses only if the brand, size, ingredient, and delivery timing meet stated needs.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** A product attribute is present in the source but is not linked to the choice.
- **Do not assume:** Listed attributes are equally important.

### Information need

- **Definition:** Knowledge a customer seeks to reduce uncertainty or make a decision.
- **Include when:** A missing question, requested detail, comparison, explanation, or guidance is explicit.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A first-time buyer needs sizing guidance before choosing a baby-care product.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** “More information would be nice” with no decision context.
- **Do not assume:** More content always improves conversion.

### Trust signal

- **Definition:** A cue used to judge reliability, authenticity, safety, suitability, or expected performance.
- **Include when:** The source connects a cue such as known brand, reviews, expert advice, seal, return policy, or prior experience to confidence.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A known brand and prior successful use make an urgent unfamiliar-category purchase acceptable.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** A five-star rating is visible but the source does not use it in the decision.
- **Do not assume:** Platform trust transfers to every product category.

### Perceived risk

- **Definition:** A customer's anticipated negative consequence or uncertainty associated with trying or choosing incorrectly.
- **Include when:** The source describes a feared loss involving money, function, quality, safety, time, authenticity, social consequence, or caregiving.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper avoids a new skincare product because an unsuitable choice could cause a reaction.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** A negative outcome is described after purchase without evidence it was anticipated beforehand.
- **Do not assume:** Objective product risk equals perceived risk.

### Shopping mission

- **Definition:** The immediate job and context organizing a shopping visit or order.
- **Include when:** The source indicates why the shopping activity occurred and relevant constraints.
- **Starter values:** Urgent top-up, routine replenishment, planned basket, stock-up, exploratory browsing, occasion-driven, lifecycle/need change, unknown.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A late-night need for one missing household item is coded as urgent top-up.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** Classifying a mission as urgent solely because the platform offers fast delivery.
- **Do not assume:** One order contains only one mission.

### Category consideration

- **Definition:** Entry, presence, or exclusion of a category from the customer's active decision set.
- **Include when:** The source shows awareness plus evaluation, deliberate ignoring, postponement, or rejection.
- **Starter values:** Not considered, noticed, evaluated, shortlisted, postponed, rejected, purchased, unknown.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper notices pet care, compares two options, then postpones purchase.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** A product impression with no evidence of attention or evaluation.
- **Do not assume:** Exposure equals consideration.

### Experiment behavior

- **Definition:** A deliberate trial of a lifetime-new, dormant, unfamiliar, or meaningfully different category/product option.
- **Include when:** Novelty relative to the customer's stated history or routine is explicit.
- **Starter values:** Considered, attempted, purchased, abandoned, repeated, not repeated, unknown.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper says this was their first pet-care purchase through quick commerce and later reports whether they repeated it.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** Buying a newly launched flavor when there is no relevance to category expansion, unless the research scope explicitly includes product-level experimentation.
- **Do not assume:** A first observed corpus mention is lifetime-new for the customer.

### Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

- **Definition:** The progress a person seeks in a specific situation, including functional, emotional, or social dimensions and current alternatives.
- **Include when:** Situation, desired progress, and outcome are sufficiently supported.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** When a routine pet product runs out unexpectedly, the shopper wants a safe replacement quickly so care is uninterrupted without researching from scratch.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** “Wants convenience” without situation, progress, or competing alternative.
- **Do not assume:** A product category itself is a job.

### Mental model

- **Definition:** A belief or internal representation used to predict what a platform, category, or purchase route is suitable for.
- **Include when:** The belief is stated or strongly evidenced across contextual behavior; label inference explicitly.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper explicitly describes quick commerce as suitable for emergency staples but not considered purchases.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** Inferring that belief from one grocery-only order.
- **Do not assume:** Behavior alone always reveals the underlying belief.

### Outcome

- **Definition:** The observable or reported result following consideration, decision, purchase, or workaround.
- **Starter values:** Purchased, abandoned, postponed, bought elsewhere, substituted, cancelled, returned/refunded, satisfied, dissatisfied, repeated, did not repeat, unknown.
- **Include when:** The result is explicitly stated or directly observable in the source.
- **Illustrative inclusion:** A shopper compares elsewhere, returns, purchases, and later repeats the category.
- **Illustrative exclusion:** Predicting repeat purchase from a positive first-purchase comment.
- **Do not assume:** Positive sentiment implies adoption or repeat behavior.

## Supporting context fields

These are descriptors, not behavioral codes:

- Source type
- Category
- Explicit segment context
- Journey stage
- Time pressure
- Prior category/channel experience
- Evidence valence: confirming, opposing, mixed, boundary case
- Interpretation certainty: explicit, inferred, unknown

## Code relationship example

Illustrative only:

```text
Shopping mission: urgent top-up
Trigger: ran out unexpectedly
Category consideration: evaluated
Perceived risk: suitability uncertainty
Information need: variant guidance
Workaround: asked a community
Outcome: bought elsewhere
```

The linked sequence is useful only when each element is supported by the same source context or clearly connected evidence.

## Pilot changes allowed

After manual pilot review, codes may be merged, split, or clarified once. Changes must record the reason and whether earlier evidence requires recoding. New codes should solve a repeated ambiguity, not describe a single anecdote.
